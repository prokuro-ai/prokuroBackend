Prokuro Subscription Pricing v1 — Reviewed Decision Brief

Date: 2026-08-14  ·  Status: Locked  ·  Do not reopen pricing. Do not change $149 / $399.

1. Executive summary

Keep Growth at $149/mo and Scale at $399/mo. Add a real Free tier with hard volume caps. Package on live BOMs plus a monthly line cap — not seats as the SKU, and not metered overages. Free proves the analyst on one small board; Growth is the working buyer (quotes); Scale is the desk (volume and orders when enabled). Enterprise stays sales-led and off the public grid. Do not advertise Free until caps, paid-priority Digi-Key, Free sync exclusion, and 402s are live.

3. Why keep $149 / $399

The stickers are already in the right B2B neighborhood. SiliconExpert’s public BOM Manager starter is $250/mo for 1 user and 1 BOM up to 500 parts. Prokuro Growth is under that price with more live BOMs and quotes. Cutting to $99 is a consumer reflex. Do not raise dollars while Digi-Key ordering is off and Growth/Scale are undifferentiated in product. Raise value on Scale (seats, 2,000-line boards, orders), not the list price. No other competitor dollar figures are used here — Z2Data and Nexar do not publish list prices we will treat as facts.

4. Packaging (locked)

Sell live BOMs + a hard monthly line cap. One sentence: you pay for how many BOMs you keep live; we cap lines so API spend cannot explode. Seats are a ceiling (1 / 2 / 5), not the SKU. Hard 402 + upgrade, not overages.

Rejected for v1: seat-primary packaging (cost is unique MPNs and analyses, not heads; hardware teams share logins) and usage overages (no meter yet; B2B buyers hate surprise invoices). Revisit overages only after 90 days of real histograms, and only for Enterprise.

5. Abuse and AWS explosion controls

Digi-Key enrichment is globally capped at 1,000 lookups/day and 120/min. Unlimited Free plus daily sync of every prokuro-parts key is how a few novel industrial BOMs starve paid customers. AWS dollars at 0–200 accounts are almost entirely lights-on (~$50–100/mo, base ~$70). Uncapped Free is a quota risk, not primarily a dollar risk.

Free kill-switches (must be live before advertising Free)

  • can_purchase=false — no quotes, no orders
  • Hard 402 at 1 BOM / 100 lines / 3 analyses / 300 lines per month
  • 1 concurrent job; 100 new MPN lookups/day/account

9. 30-day metrics and red flags

Watch weekly. Decide on day 30. Do not change $149 / $399 during the experiment unless a red flag below fires.

Metrics

  • Signups (accounts created)
  • Free → paid conversion: ≥8% of Free that ran ≥1 analysis
  • Free cap-hit rate: 20–40% of actives (too low = Free too fat)
  • Analyses / active user (Free vs Growth)
  • Lines / analysis (p50 / p90)
  • AWS bill vs ~$70 baseline
  • Digi-Key / enrichment 429s and paid-queue wait
  • Quote attempts on Free (succeeding should be ~0)
  • Stripe: new Growth vs Scale; monthly vs annual

Red flags

Tighten Free immediately (do not wait 30 days): AWS bill >$150 with <20 paid; Digi-Key 429s starving paid analyses; daily sync growing from Free-only MPNs; one domain burning the 400/day Free intake; Free users running 500+ line production BOMs via split files or re-signup.

Raise price only after 30 days: conversion >25% and paid users sitting >80% of caps without churn; Scale attaching with no push. Do not raise if conversion <5% — tighten Free and upgrade copy first.

Decision. This brief is locked. Implement entitlements and kill-switches, then annual Stripe, then the public grid. Pricing without caps is how TEST becomes an AWS/API incident.
