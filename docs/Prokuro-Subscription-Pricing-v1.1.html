3. Digi-Key 50,000 / day · 480 / min

Account billing@prokuro.ai: 50,000 lookups/day, 480/min. v1 sized Free as if the limiter were 1,000/day and 120/min. Those numbers are obsolete for planning. They are still what the code enforces in crates/prokuroEnrichment/src/providers/digikey/rate_limit.rs (MAX_PER_DAY=1000, MAX_PER_MINUTE=120). Ship order step 0 is to raise the limiter to 50,000 / 480 before opening Free or daily paid sync, or the new budgets will 429 against our own code.

Keep-forever catalog does not mean sync every MPN ever seen every day. At 50k/day, a 100k-part historical table would never catch up. Daily job order: (1) MPNs on paid active BOMs, (2) remaining quota for catalog freshness, (3) Free weekly refresh of the one active BOM. Nexar, when added, follows the same paid-daily / Free-weekly split.

4. Data: keep forever, gate refresh

S3 BOM artifacts and Dynamo MPN snapshots are cheap. Deleting Free data was the wrong control: it destroys the component network we are building and removes the conversion surface (their board is already in the product). v1.1: never TTL-delete account BOMs or MPN cache for quota reasons.

  • Over active-BOM cap: keep the extra files; stop refreshing them; UI: stored, not monitored.
  • Free stale: after 7 days without a weekly slot, show a stale banner (“Availability last refreshed n days ago — Growth keeps this live daily”). Data remains.
  • Downgrade / cancel: keep data; drop to Free refresh and Free purchasing caps; do not wipe.
  • S3: expire noncurrent versions at 30 days (cost hygiene), not current objects.

Conversion hook is live monitoring, not an empty Free tier.

5. Bedrock policy

Analyst/agentic path is the moat. Growth and Scale: Bedrock on (Haiku default; tighter token budgets than Sonnet). Free: Bedrock on but lighter — Haiku only, no recursive alternate expansion, counted inside the 3 analyses / 300 lines, max 50k tokens per analysis. Paid: Bedrock on for analysis and agent steps, still bounded by line/analysis caps. Do not run Sonnet on every line. Alarm Bedrock $ in CloudWatch/Cost Explorer. This is a cost watch, not a reason to cut list price.

6. Cost snapshot (order of magnitude, USD)

Lights-on AWS still ~$50–100/mo (base ~$70). One Growth subscriber still covers baseline. Digi-Key cash remains $0 at this contract; the scarce resource is 50k/day, not dollars. 9. 30-day metrics, CloudWatch, red flags

Watch weekly. Retune caps and copy on day 30. Do not change $149 / $399 unless a red flag below fires.

Product / billing counters (gateway + Dynamo)

  • Signups; Free → paid among Free that ran ≥1 analysis (target ≥8%)
  • 402 cap hits by cap name (lines, analyses, purchasing actions, MPN/day)
  • Free cap-hit rate 20–40% of actives
  • Analyses / active user; lines / analysis (p50 / p90)
  • Purchasing actions / user (Free vs paid) — watch scraping
  • Stale-banner impressions vs Growth checkout
  • Stripe: Growth vs Scale; monthly vs annual

CloudWatch native

  • AWS estimated charges: alert $150, panic $250
  • Bedrock InvocationModel $ / token counts (new vs v1)
  • Logs IncomingBytes; keep 1-day Fargate retention; alarm > 5 GB/day
  • ECS RunningTaskCount > 2
  • Digi-Key 429s / rate-limit errors on enrichment (should be rare at 50k if limiter matches contract)
  • ALB 5xx; gateway 402 count as a custom metric
  • Dynamo consumed RCU/WCU on prokuro-parts (catalog growth)

Tighten Free immediately: AWS bill >$150 with <20 paid; Bedrock $ surprising vs Haiku model; Digi-Key 429s starving paid daily sync; one domain burning the 5,000/day Free intake; Free purchasing actions used as a quote-scrape farm (many accounts, 5 actions each, no analyses); Free users splitting 500-line production BOMs. Raise price only after 30 days if conversion >25% and paid users sit >80% of caps without churn. Do not raise if conversion <5% — tighten Free refresh/actions and the stale-banner upgrade copy first.

Decision. v1.1 is locked. Prices unchanged. Implement limiter 50k/480, keep-forever storage, weekly Free / daily paid refresh, Bedrock on paid, Free purchasing under 5 actions. Then annual Stripe, then the public grid.
